# E13 An event time the receiver's record can't hold

## Meaning

The failed operation's event time doesn't fit the encounter: it is before the patient's admission, more than five
minutes after the message was sent (MSH-7) or the evidence was taken, more than a day from the time the PAS recorded
the event (EVN-2), or within the hour around a clock change where a local time without an offset can't be ordered.
Shutter orders and supersedes movements by event time, so a wrong time can resend a movement that predates the stay,
or make genuine failures look overtaken.

Such a time is usually not simply bad data. Clinicians often digitise paper records retrospectively: the notes are
typed in hours or days after the event, so the event time (EVN-6) is genuine and lies far before the time the PAS
recorded it (EVN-2). The other common cause is a clock fault, in the PAS, the integration engine or the device that
stamped the event, which gives times in the future or before the admission. Which of these it is, and whether the
movement should still be sent, can't be told from the messages, and Shutter doesn't correct either: the case goes to a
person with the times named, who decides with the PAS team checking the source.

## Telling it apart

A movement overtaken by a later one with a sound time is E1; a failed movement still due is E2. Here the time itself
is the question. When the time is plausible but another source moved the patient at the same minute, it is E7.

## Cite

The failed operation's event time, the admission time (PV1-44 or the receiver's first history entry), EVN-2, MSH-7,
and the feed's time zone setting.
